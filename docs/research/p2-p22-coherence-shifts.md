# P2-P22 — Coherence during decision formation and across cases

Holyoak & Simon (1999), *JEP: General* 128(1):3–31, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.128.1.3), [filed PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1vdn9H7rtsgStuJdup0IhUL8JSMEessfR/view). Experiment 1, Fig. 2 and its surrounding results, found the largest change in argument ratings by the interim measurement in the three-phase condition, before formal verdict. The interim/posttest contrast was not statistically significant; that does not establish identical scores. Experiment 2, Fig. 5, reproduced diverging assessments after a one-week interval (19 participants) and tested recall. Experiment 3 studied transfer to a second legal scenario and distinguishes initial-case conditions from the transfer-case pattern (Figs. 6–8 and accompanying analyses). The early change challenges a story that requires the final announcement to cause all reweighting.

The authors already supply a computational constraint-satisfaction model. A NOMOS simulation would require a source-trace question or distinct prediction. Experiments do not directly observe how a formally withdrawn record enters later institutional reasons.
