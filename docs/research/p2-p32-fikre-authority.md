# P2-P32 — Fikre's notice identifies a channel

[No. 22-1178 petition, App. F ¶4 and App. G](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1cWt-5FuX3MZ8wLY8sojiFfGMqbTS0eqg/view#page=150) · [601 U.S. 234](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1HulDICGotzXDQYyeDkMj_OW1LidBjC-U/view).

The March 2015 order is an operative TSA order. The Courtright declaration identifies one May 6, 2016 reporting chain: TSC informed government counsel of the removal and counsel told Fikre's counsel. It does not identify the person or office signing an operative removal order, the legal route that produced the removal, or its grounds. The Supreme Court noted that the communication was unaccompanied by an explanation and cited ECF 98, p. 1. Use the observation as a delisting and communicated status. Keep the decider of the individual delisting unresolved.

The earlier audit left the pair unclassified. Shorthand attributing the operative removal to TSC is too strong. [Kashem's 2019 reconstruction](p2-p32-kashem-redress.md) describes a revised administrative route and distinguishes TSC's recommendation and TSA's final administrative adjudication. The actual route in Fikre remains unverified. For the pairing input, use a status notice with an unknown deciding authority. Mark a second decision only where the operative record supports it.
