# P2-P22 — Belief perseverance after discrediting

Anderson, Lepper & Ross (1980), *JPSP* 39(6):1037–1049, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077720), [filed PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mLyoR0IKymxcpgq_hH6nIH8UOvIxGXjh/view). Two case histories initially point to opposite risk-taking associations with success as a firefighter. Experiment 1 distinguishes discrediting the ability feedback from discrediting the entire case history. Experiment 2 (p. 1043, Fig. 2 and pp. 1044–1045) used 62 Stanford undergraduates and manipulated an explicit explanation task. Among the six cells, the no-debriefing comparison had no prompted explanation. This is not a fully crossed debriefing-by-explanation design. Experiment 2's within-explanation correlation was not significant (p. 1045 n. 5). The reported between-condition result and that within-condition result answer different questions.

The cases were disclosed as fictitious; the authors also discuss how participants understood the disclosure (p. 1045). A generated explanation can survive discrediting, and the evidence must distinguish an explanation prompted by a defeated source from an independently supported reason. This study measures personal beliefs and generalization, not reasons accepted by an institution. NOMOS should not claim belief perseverance or causal-scenario generation as original.
