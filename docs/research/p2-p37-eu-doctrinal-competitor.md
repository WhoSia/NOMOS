# P2-P37 supplementary rival — EU law already reaches upstream scoring

**Status:** Source-read doctrinal comparator, 2026-11-21. **Held originals:** [Binns & Veale 2021](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qCMJU9E8GYEp0OC4-dnZXuAHj3b01G1_/view); [CJEU C-634/21, SCHUFA 7 Dec 2023](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mwHncHICL3r36MO4HmsyExK9yAsTFazn/view); [CJEU C-203/22, Dun & Bradstreet 27 Feb 2025](https://drive.google.com/file/d/120ArOyU0TsxsQ7brWp0o0srmCoeUss6F/view). Each case is interpreted on its distinct facts, questions and operative part.

## The strongest novelty defeater

Binns and Veale's 2021 article theorizes that selective automation and intermediate triage can meaningfully shape a person's path even where a later human nominally makes the decision. That is an antecedent research argument, not a holding about every multi-stage system.

In **SCHUFA (C-634/21)**, the CJEU's operative ruling interprets GDPR Article 22(1) to include a credit information agency's automated construction of a probability value about an individual's future payment capacity **where the third party to which the score is transmitted draws strongly on that value** in establishing, performing or terminating a contractual relationship. It is not a holding that all scoring is Article 22 automated decision-making regardless of downstream reliance. The interpretation and national-implementation requirements must be checked in the relevant actual process.

In **Dun & Bradstreet Austria (C-203/22)**, the CJEU's first operative paragraph interprets Article 15(1)(h) in the specified automated-decision context: provide the data subject with concise, transparent, intelligible, accessible and relevant information explaining the *procedure and principles actually applied* to their personal data to reach a particular result (e.g. credit profile). Its second paragraph requires a controller asserting third-party data protection or trade secrecy to submit the allegedly protected material to the competent regulator or court for a balancing of interests, **not** to assume an automatic veto to disclosure nor categorical public release of secrets.

This existing 2023/2025 doctrine already treats an upstream score as potentially decision-significant, and recognizes access to reasoning about a specific result. Thus NOMOS cannot promote that general insight to original discovery, or replace GDPR with a private conceptual scoring system.

## Distinctions still worth auditing

| Question | Existing legal or research baseline | Possible NOMOS operational contribution (unvalidated) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is an ADM actor when score strongly shapes downstream decision? | C-634/21 expressly recognizes conditional upstream Article 22 reach. | Graph of actual packet transmission, recipient-specific role and observable reliance, without inferring every score actually meets the test. |
| What explanation can a data subject seek? | C-203/22 requires intelligible information about actually applied procedure/principles, with protected-content review by regulator/court. | Check whether a response concerns this person's version and outcome, not solely generic method documentation. |
| Does an error correction propagate to the real recipient? | Existing FCRA/GDPR procedures and ordinary data-lineage audits offer important prior mechanisms. | An inspectable source-to-recipient evidence chain, if it beats equal-access baselines at rejecting unsupported claims. |
| Did the recipient make a distinct, legitimate, corrected decision? | Authorization, grounds of review and actual effects depend on the governing jurisdiction and record. | Separate the authenticity of an act, its reasons, authorized use and the external consequence in a cross-domain audit. |

**Transport controls:** EU GDPR holdings are not governing law for US credit-reporting, US aviation cases, or Australian administrative remedies. An explanation request, a transfer of a score, strong reliance on that score, and real-world correction are not identical events.

## Falsification test

Give legally trained baseline reviewers both CJEU operative parts and the NOMOS packet. If they identify upstream influence, access-to-logic rights and the absence of demonstrated post-correction person-level effects as accurately as NOMOS, **discard any legal-theory novelty claim**. NOMOS might still be a useful reproducible evidence-audit convention, but that claim also needs comparative measurement. Do not score ordinary lawyers as wrong for withholding a claim that the underlying recipient process has not actually been observed.
