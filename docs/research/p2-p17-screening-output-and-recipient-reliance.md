## Small formal check: does a report-side change identify a decision-side change?

Let s and t represent the presence of an adverse signal in the primary and an alternative channel available to a recipient. Among otherwise-eligible cases assume a deterministic monotone binary denial rule f with f(0,0)=0 and f(1,1)=1. The two free truth-table entries f(0,1), f(1,0) give exactly four admissible rules. The table was evaluated by enumerating both binary inputs for each rule. This is a constructed comparison; no actual SafeRent workflow or applicant is encoded.

| Recipient rule | Both present | Primary suppressed | Alternative suppressed | Both suppressed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| primary | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| alternative | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| either | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| both | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

A primary-signal suppression can be verified from a valid baseline and post-change report. Holding the alternative signal present, the benefit is unidentified from the reports alone: two admissible rules reverse the decision, two retain it. The 2/4 is a count of rules under this construction and supplies no probability or observed proportion. Denial after suppression narrows the rules in this model but does not identify whether the recipient newly switched to another channel, used it all along, or relied on other facts in real settings. Report-level suppression may still independently advance privacy and reduce future exposure. It does not license indiscriminate further suppression or treating all remaining signals as legally or empirically interchangeable.

## Discriminating evidence

For report-side implementation, seek certification plus sampled reports, their product configuration, qualifying voucher status, source labels and any applicable validation. For decision-side analysis, seek dated recipient reasons, evidence that the decision incorporates the remaining signal, and ideally independent policy or process evidence showing what would happen without that signal. Compare a rule that uses a substitute proxy, a rule that uses relevant independent information, and a rule that changes through reconsideration/appeal. A repeated denial alone is ambiguous. Under an identical case packet, ask a legal/contextual-integrity/relational baseline and NOMOS to identify those distinctions and their evidence requirements. No comparative advantage or field outcome is established.
