# P2-P25 — The two intervention studies in Ko's dissertation

[Ko (2016), pp. 59–116, 127–134 and Appendix 9](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YtQ0okj_40wKVt2CxBfW8SlSsAtID9SW/view#page=74).

Study 3-1 used the negative-character stimulus. It compares discussion, counterfactual reasoning plus discussion, and counterfactual reasoning plus anonymous peer evaluation against a control. The counterfactual task asks participants to write reasons for both their initial and opposite verdicts. The discussion-only groups had roughly 40 minutes and the counterfactual-plus-discussion groups roughly 20 minutes of discussion. The total in Table 13 is 246 (62, 65, 62, 57), including 121 students and 125 general-population participants in the method. The means move from 58.87 to 46.24, 58.35 to 52.66, 58.27 to 49.77, and 58.91 to 61.65, respectively. The procedure identifies its control as the Study 1 negative condition (p. 70), whose table instead reports 26 and 61.08 to 63.19. The population-specific control rows in Tables 14 and 18 warrant treating the pooled control's assembly as unresolved. The comparisons confound several intervention features. The author reported a nonsignificant three-way interaction of population, condition and time on p. 75. Different within-population significance patterns do not by themselves identify heterogeneity.

The control subgroups total 57 (27 students and 30 nonstudents). Their size-weighted means are approximately 60.28 initially and 61.65 finally, compared with the pooled row's 58.91 and 61.65. The initial discrepancy exceeds any two-decimal rounding. Use Table 13 as reported, with no reconstruction of the missing allocation.

Study 3-2 inserted a specific instruction between initial judgment and evaluation of the two sides. Appendix 9 directs participants to set aside the defendant's character, reputation and personality, then decide using the other information. This is a change in permitted use within the experiment. It does not tell participants that a prior fact was false. The instructed negative cell shifts 58.44→50.16 and the instructed positive cell 39.91→56.24. The final between-valence comparison under instruction is nonsignificant. The three-way valence-by-instruction-by-time interaction was reported as significant (pp. 109–111).

Methods name Study 1 as the no-instruction comparison (p. 108). Table 22's column headings print 26, 33, 32 and 33 for instructed-negative, instructed-positive, no-instruction-negative and no-instruction-positive. This sums to 124 against 119 in the methods. Table 23 reports 32, 33, 26 and 28 in that same ordering (total 119). These also agree with the instructed paired-test degrees of freedom (31 and 32) and the no-instruction means' Study 1 matches. It is reasonable to use 119 as the reported experiment total while keeping the inconsistent printed column headers visible. Participant records would be required for a row-level reconciliation. The model must also allow a justified fact to lose permission for a particular use without treating its content as false.

The general discussion keeps the single-case stimulus and comprehension of jury directions among its limits. Mood, burden of proof, verdict accuracy, admissibility in an actual case and the institutional aftermath of a corrected record are distinct endpoints.
