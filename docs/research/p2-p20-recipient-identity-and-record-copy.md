# P2-P20 — Recipient identity and copies of personal data

**C‑154/21, Österreichische Post, ECLI:EU:C:2023:3, 12 January 2023.** The operative part interpreting Article 15(1)(c) requires actual recipient identities if data have been or will be disclosed, except where identification is impossible or the controller demonstrates that the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive. The recipient list can help target a correction and evaluate the effect of disclosure. It does not by itself give the delivery or use history of a particular corrected version. [Judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A62021CJ0154)

**C‑487/21, Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde and CRIF, ECLI:EU:C:2023:369, 4 May 2023.** The first operative point and paras. 32–45 require a faithful, intelligible copy of personal data undergoing processing. Document or database extracts and, if essential, entire documents can be part of that copy, with others' rights and freedoms accounted for. It is not an unrestricted demand for the entire report system. Record identity and context are evidentiary aids to check the inputs or compare versions. The next question is whether the past recipient received and actually relied on the revised version. [Judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=ecli%3AECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2023%3A369)

Article 19 communication is a different event. Store separate fields for identified recipient, dispatch, confirmed receipt and substantively recorded use. An institutional assertion that recipients are known does not warrant a delivery assertion. [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)
