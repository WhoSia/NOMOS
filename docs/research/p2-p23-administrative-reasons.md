# P2-P23 — Same practical result and different administrative reasons

## Positive matched comparison: Chenery I and II

*SEC v. Chenery*, [318 U.S. 80, 86–89 (1943)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep318/usrep318080/usrep318080.pdf), describes an SEC order whose stated basis relied on doctrines the agency drew from judicial equity precedents. The Court found those precedents insufficient to support that order. It did not resolve the merits on possible grounds the SEC had not invoked.

*SEC v. Chenery*, [332 U.S. 194, 197–203 (1947)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep332/usrep332194/usrep332194.pdf), explains the fresh reorganization-related application and 8 February 1945 SEC order (Holding Company Act Release 5584; 18 S.E.C. 767). The Court describes a new examination based on §§7 and 11 and SEC experience and sustained that exercise of authority. At the level of management's parity with other preferred shareholders, the practical exclusion remained. The formal application and order changed. The cited pages are the two Supreme Court opinions, not independently read copies of SEC orders 10 S.E.C. 200 and 18 S.E.C. 767. This is judicial rejection of a justificatory ground, not correction of a factual record or an assessment of personal moral character.

## Negative control: explanatory memorandum without new action

[Duke memorandum, 5 September 2017](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/F02BE672-B2E4-4243-8D05-8C811397E534/17090532.pdf), archived DHS text reproduced by AILA. [Nielsen memorandum, 22 June 2018, pp. 1–3](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/publications/18_0622_S1_Memorandum_DACA.pdf), preserves Duke's action and offers illegality, doubts about legality, and enforcement-policy grounds. [DHS v. Regents, majority slip op. 13–17 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-587_5ifl.pdf), distinguishes further explanation of the original reasons from genuinely new agency action and treats the new policy reasons as unavailable to sustain the old rescission. Part III.B considers omitted alternatives and reliance. Record the Court's legal assessment separately from the memo's self-description of independent sufficiency. This is a policy decision affecting a cohort. It does not expose facts about an individual or a decisionmaker's private reasoning.

## Decision for NOMOS

These pairs already supply a strong legal baseline for when a ground can be assessed and a fresh action can reach a similar practical result. An institutional reason audit cannot count the basic contrast as a NOMOS novelty. Its next claim must concern some documentable distinction such as whether a cited successor source descends from an item expressly withdrawn in the same case. Without the underlying record lineage the relation is unobserved.
