# P2-P33 — Ibrahim's compliance declarations and a distinct visa refusal

[Ibrahim v. DHS, N.D. Cal. No. 3:06-cv-00545-WHA, ECF 736–737 and exhibits](https://papersplease.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/ibrahim-declarations-15apr2014.pdf), filed April 15, 2014. The district court's [January 14 merits order](p2-p33-ibrahim-findings.md) defines the scope of the correction.

ECF 736 p. 2 reports compliance. ECF 737 ¶¶2–6 introduces the five agency declarations in Exs. 1–5. The declarations give different accounts of searches, deletions and retention with explicit corrections. For example, Ex. 1 ¶¶8–11 distinguishes annotated paper records from an electronic reference the agency reports removing. Ex. 2 ¶¶8–13 separates current records from annotated archival copies. These are agency accounts of work within their declared scope. Independent checks of the modified records and evidence of use by later recipients remain open.

Ex. 6 ¶¶2–3 and Ex. A reproduce a visa refusal dated April 14, 2014, cite a statutory ground and describe eligibility to apply for a discretionary waiver. The refusal came after the January order and before the April 15 compliance filing. The exhibits identify a date, a stated ground and the decision channel. They leave open the completion date of the particular correction relevant to that decision, which record version the deciding official saw, and actual reliance. The [Federal Judicial Center case study](https://www.fjc.gov/sites/default/files/2015/National%20Security%20Case%20Studies%20Sixth%20Edition%20Reagan%202015.pdf) discusses the separation of visa and no-fly matters.

ECF 737 ¶¶7–8 and Exs. 7–8 report the April 15 delivery to counsel of distinct visa and No Fly List materials. The FBI status letter reports the listing status. The documented recipient of the transmission is counsel. The [2019 en banc opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2019/01/02/14-16161.pdf) addresses attorney fees and reviews the case history.
