# NOMOS-0.885 P2-P15 — Tenant screening, remedy implementation and later decisions

2026-11-14. Follow-up to P2-P14's strong relational/legal baseline. These are public-document case studies reviewed by the same researcher. They are suitable for building a benchmark packet, not for estimating performance or generalizing to all recipients.

## CoreLogic: the person received permission, the propagation path is unresolved

In *Connecticut Fair Housing Center v. CoreLogic*, Nos. 23-1118 and 23-1166 (2d Cir., Feb. 20, 2026), pp. 8–11, WinnResidential denied Mikhail's application on April 26, 2016 in connection with the CrimSAFE report. In December, Arroyo learned the report concerned a pending Pennsylvania shoplifting charge. In April 2017, the Pennsylvania court informed her that the charge had been withdrawn. Arroyo also sought a reasonable accommodation through the Connecticut Commission on Human Rights and Opportunities (CHRO). The CHRO held an evidentiary hearing June 13, 2017. Ten days later WinnResidential permitted Mikhail to move into ArtSpace. This documents a person-specific change in authorization. Physical move-in, the timing of the withdrawal itself, and receipt, use or regeneration of a revised CrimSAFE report are distinct claims.

A corrected-report path and an accommodation/reconsideration path can both coexist with the published chronology. So can a decision that used new source information communicated outside the vendor. The opinion does not identify which would have been sufficient or necessary. Priority evidence is the CHRO case disposition/settlement (subject to lawful public access), the recipient's dated decision and reasons, and a versioned replacement report with a receipt or a decision record showing use. An explicit reason for the later decision is more probative than proximity to a hearing.

Do not import the appellate FHA result as a finding about the cause of the later authorization. The panel rejected categorical exclusion of screening vendors, upheld the failure of case-specific FHA causation for Arroyo, vacated the organizational merits for lack of standing, and reversed the FCRA consumer-file disclosure liability. See opinion pp. 2–3 and Discussion. [Opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca2-23-01166/pdf/USCOURTS-ca2-23-01166-0.pdf).

## Comparison with prior art and next test

Selbst et al. explain why changing a technical input can miss an institution's actual decision process (especially framing and ripple effects). Wachter and Mittelstadt's proposed right to reasonable inferences already distinguishes upstream inputs and ex ante justification of downstream inference, including relevance, normative permissibility and reliability, as well as ex post challenge. Kaminski and Urban analyze individual contestation, and Kaminski's binary governance includes systemic oversight. Ustun et al. distinguish feasible feature changes under a classifier from mere explanations. Karimi et al. study interventions on causal systems. Neither method by itself identifies what an actual landlord later received or acted on. Prescott and Starr's Michigan evidence reports uptake around 6.5% within five years among eligible individuals, and wage changes after expungement. Population-level outcome estimates answer a different question from the transmission path for one record and recipient.

Candidate contribution: compare the unit of each remedy (source record, report, score, money or decision), who can perform it, and which dated observation could resolve downstream reliance. Corporate self-report, administrator issuance, signed court order and actual recipient action are different evidence types. Several can be true with different values for the missing decision edge. CFPB's market report and consumer snapshot describe market structure and reported experiences respectively. The snapshot does not supply a population prevalence denominator for the complaints it illustrates.

Next paired test: provide identical dated, excerpted packets to R1 (expert legal + contextual integrity + relational governance) and NOMOS, preserving case identifiers and authority type. Pre-register four endpoint-specific questions: verified later authorization, verified version receipt/use, documented monetary implementation by source class, and reason for the later decision. Mask the final reference annotations, independently adjudicate each answer, and measure unsupported positive claims and correct abstentions as well as resolved claims. Include controls with an authenticated receipt but no subsequent decision, and a documented appeal reversal with no data edit. If both methods obtain the same endpoint-level judgments, the pilot supports using the checklist but supplies no evidence of comparative advantage. No independent benchmark was run in this round.
