# P2-P25 — The 2016 dissertation and 2019 article report the same Study 1 table

Read the original [Ko Minjo dissertation](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YtQ0okj_40wKVt2CxBfW8SlSsAtID9SW/view), Seoul National University PhD in psychology (2016), pp. 24–39, and the [Ko Minjo and Park Jooyong article](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iJxO19salkx9yQGfArAEhW9G3kYgaUGl/view), pp. 215, 220–225. The article's first-page footnote states that the lead author's 2016 doctoral data were reused. This is direct evidence of dependency at the study-report level.

| Condition | Participants | Initial mean (SD) | Final mean (SD) |

All four counts, eight means and eight standard deviations match in the dissertation's Table 4 (p. 32) and the article's Table 3 (p. 223). Both report 92 participants in the method (dissertation p. 27 and journal p. 220), 124 in the results table, and residual degrees of freedom of 120 in the four-group test (dissertation p. 31 and article pp. 222–223). The three groups with character information sum exactly to 92. The two method sections and Study 1 appendices establish the control stimulus and intended procedure, while the neutral group's recruitment, assignment, and any exclusions or pooling remain unresolved. The journal reports random assignment across four conditions in the adjacent stimulus column. Obtain a published correction or authorized aggregate flow statement before reconciling the method and table as a single recruitment claim. Source-row identity has not been assessed.

The stimulus adapted one Korean circumstantial-evidence murder case, ultimately an acquittal. The simulation varied five bundled biographical cues. Participants entered an initial binary verdict and probability, considered both sides on ten issues, then entered a final verdict and probability. The reported positive group rated guilt lower than the other groups at both time points. The reported interaction between condition and time was nonsignificant. The initial-verdict strata compared on later reason ratings (Table 7 and Appendices 3–5) are groups created by participants' own verdicts. Those contrasts document an association and cannot identify an individual mediation effect. The simulation did not withdraw a false source, test a later authorized institutional use, or directly observe reliance on a versioned record.
