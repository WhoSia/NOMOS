# P2-P25 — The 2016 dissertation and 2019 article report the same Study 1 table

Read the original [Ko Minjo dissertation](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YtQ0okj_40wKVt2CxBfW8SlSsAtID9SW/view), Seoul National University PhD in psychology (2016), pp. 24–39, and the [Ko Minjo and Park Jooyong article](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iJxO19salkx9yQGfArAEhW9G3kYgaUGl/view), pp. 215, 220–225. The article's first-page footnote states that the lead author's 2016 doctoral data were reused. This is direct evidence of dependency at the study-report level.

The three groups with character information sum exactly to 92. The two method sections and Study 1 appendices establish the control stimulus and intended procedure, while the neutral group's recruitment, assignment, and any exclusions or pooling remain unresolved. The journal reports random assignment across four conditions in the adjacent stimulus column. Obtain a published correction or authorized aggregate flow statement before reconciling the method and table as a single recruitment claim. Source-row identity has not been assessed.

## P2-P39 image-page verification and classification correction (2026-11-21)

The searchable layer in the 217-page PhD scan misses much body text, so **source images were re-rendered and inspected**, rather than attempting to infer the method from OCR. Printed p. 27 (PDF page 42) explicitly says **92** students, including 47 men and 45 women, mean age 21.23 (SD=2.6). Printed p. 28 (PDF page 43), Table 2, explicitly displays **four** conditions including a neutral/no-person-info control. Printed p. 29 (PDF page 44) explicitly describes mixed-factor analysis with four levels and two verdict times; printed p. 30 (PDF page 45), Figure 4, displays assignment to four stimulus variants before initial/issue/final verdict ratings. Printed p. 31 (PDF page 46) gives **F(3,120)=5.130** for character-evidence condition, confirming analysis consistent with total N=124. Printed p. 32 (PDF page 47), Table 4, gives **26 + 28 + 38 + 32 = 124**. The first three group counts add to 92 **arithmetically**, but nothing in the inspected methods demonstrates that the 32 were recruited independently, omitted from demographics by design, added later, or that the authors made a simple typographical mistake. These remain distinguishable hypotheses, not established history.

**Status:** both primary reports are HELD; the outstanding item is an **unresolved reporting/sampling discrepancy**, not an unowned or missing dissertation. An author erratum, investigator clarification or sufficiently detailed participant-flow provenance could resolve it, if such records exist. Until then, retain both denominators with their source locators and do not report a confidently reconciled N. The existence of an independent neutral cohort is not established.

The stimulus adapted one Korean circumstantial-evidence murder case, ultimately an acquittal. The simulation varied five bundled biographical cues. Participants entered an initial binary verdict and probability, considered both sides on ten issues, then entered a final verdict and probability. The reported positive group rated guilt lower than the other groups at both time points. The reported interaction between condition and time was nonsignificant. The initial-verdict strata compared on later reason ratings (Table 7 and Appendices 3–5) are groups created by participants' own verdicts. Those contrasts document an association and cannot identify an individual mediation effect. The simulation did not withdraw a false source, test a later authorized institutional use, or directly observe reliance on a versioned record.
