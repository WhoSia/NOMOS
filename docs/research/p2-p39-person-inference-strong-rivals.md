# NOMOS-0.885 P2-P39 — The person-inference problem against strong psychological and philosophical rivals

**Date:** 2026-11-21. **Status:** source rereading and adversarial conceptual analysis; no new formal version, user study, or measured experimental superiority. This is a public statement of arguments, *not* a source-custody register.

## The original question

Camus's *L'Étranger* makes it necessary to ask whether a bounded observation of conduct legitimately supports a broad and durable judgment about a person, and who is authorized to make that judgment. Separate **observational support**, **interpretive scope**, **legal admissibility**, **social credibility**, and **institutional consequence**. The five are not interchangeable, and an apparently morally troubling judgment is not automatically a false factual claim.

Three problems demand different theories, even if they interact:

1. **Persistence of a belief whose evidentiary support has been explicitly retracted.** A person may continue to hold an impression after its supporting data are debunked.
2. **Persistence of an evidentiary influence after a legal exclusion instruction.** Information can be *true* and relevant to ordinary thinking, yet inadmissible for the task.
3. **Construction or preservation of a person-wide trait or group stereotype despite conflicting conduct.** A counterexample may be reinterpreted as exceptional rather than changing the stereotype.

A dated agency correction is a fourth problem: to show that an actual downstream decision relied on the old basis, one needs the corrected source, identified recipient, receipt/version, new decision and stated reason. Psychology experiments cannot supply those missing institutional observations.

**Critical reading of Steblay (2006):** The original article reports 48 studies drawn from 42 papers and 175 nonindependent tests (pp. 475 onward); it warns that moderator categories overlap and some contain few tests (Tables 1–2). Figure 2 and the subset analyses are not interchangeable with a claim about every evidence type or every juror. Even a reasoned exclusion instruction need not undo exposure, and a theory of inadmissibility differs from a theory of empirical falsification. This distinction produces a negative control rather than an argument for inevitable cognitive bias.

## The strong-rival comparison

| Situation | Narrowly licensed inference | Extra claim requiring separate support |
|---|---|---|
| A prosecutor criticizes a defendant's conduct in its proper legal context | The conduct can be evaluated, including relevant contextual and relational duties | That the episode constitutes a stable overall character, or independently proves the charged act |
| A philosopher rejects a paper because it demonstrably violates a stated method | The paper has a task-relative flaw | The author is unfit to think philosophically in any domain |
| A juror hears material later declared inadmissible | The exposure may shape responses, as in Steblay | The material was false, or the verdict was *actually* caused by it |
| A recipient issues a second denial after a correction | The outcome may have remained adverse | The recipient used a stale copy, unless receipt and reason dependence are actually documented |
| A reviewer retains the same outcome citing a **genuinely independent** valid reason | Same result after correction is logically possible without continued old-source reliance | Calling that lawful independent judgment proof of failed correction |

**Research criterion:** NOMOS must add more than a renaming of well-known belief perseverance, exclusion-instruction effects, stereotyping or social epistemic injustice. Its proposed contribution is a common *scope-of-warrant* question: at which step does justified criticism of an event or claim get used as permission to classify the whole bearer, and what additional institutional authority supports that transition? This is a **candidate synthesis**, not an established new discovery.

## Pre-registered adversarial cases, no observations fabricated

The first negative control is a properly supported task-relative rejection (no unjust person-wide judgment). The second is a legitimate independent post-correction reason (same result, no stale dependence). The third is a court that distinguishes a separate decision from a notice or explanation. A methodological rival that reaches the same answers with the same primary texts defeats any assertion of unique NOMOS advantage.

The prospective design has two non-equivalent parts. In literary analysis: close read Meursault's funeral, caretaker, Pérez and Raymond episodes without inferring jurors' inner beliefs or removing the victim. In empirical work: freeze source packets, conceal framework labels, and measure unsupported transition claims under matched access and time; do **not** call observed psychological ratings a direct measure of formal legal authority.
