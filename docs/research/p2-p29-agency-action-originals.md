# P2-P29 — Original instruments and later reasons

The [Duke memorandum](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ZKHmeAQzCgvULtx1WOoDg4ilyGZFyJ8V/view), [Nielsen memorandum](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1lshtNoR0RL5Kw5d3LyvGsIO3ptr8XxQh/view), and [Regents slip-opinions packet](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mBpgu_KpXPbjqah28eyKKCVqUr-42p6k/view) recover the source documents for the P2-P23 contrast.

| Record | Operative role, with locator |
|---|---|
| Duke, 5 September 2017, DHS web print pp. 2, 4–6 | Rescinds the named 2012 memorandum and prescribes a wind-down, with categories and dates for pending and renewal requests. The legal analysis points to related DAPA litigation and the Attorney General's letter. |
| Nielsen, 22 June 2018, memorandum pp. 1–3 after the archive cover | Declines to disturb Duke's rescission. Supplies lack of statutory authority, legal uncertainty and enforcement-policy grounds. The memo discusses reliance on p. 3. Reading this as a fresh decision would erase its own description of its role. |
| *Regents*, 18 June 2020, majority slip pp. 13–20 | The majority accepts contemporaneous elaboration or a new decision, and excludes materially new later grounds for the existing action. It distinguishes continuing forbearance from eligibility for benefits and separately addresses reliance. Kavanaugh dissents on the treatment of the Nielsen memo (opinion pp. 3–11). |
| *Chenery I*, 318 U.S. 80, 86–89, 94–95; *Chenery II*, 332 U.S. 194, 197–203 | The first opinion rejects the basis actually relied on in the SEC order. The second reviews a later order supported on newly articulated agency grounds. The contemporary U.S. Reports scans are now both held. The full independent SEC orders remain an acquisition gap. |

A same-outcome comparison has two separate units: stated grounds in a later document and operative decisions. A later document with more reasons can be genuine additional reasoning without ending the identity of the underlying act. A new act can reach the same disposition. Register the role and decision identifier before applying a reason-transition comparison. The legal comparator already supplies this distinction, and it is absorbed as a baseline.
