# P2-P32 — Kashem on who recommends, decides and discloses

[Kashem v. Barr, No. 17-35634, held slip opinion](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KsbFwnZpv5STfrIqD6CF-8riQmnUVVSW/view) · [Ninth Circuit PDF](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2019/10/21/17-35634.pdf) · 941 F.3d 358 (2019).

- Slip pp. 7–11 and 57–60 distinguish the roles. In pre-2015 traveler redress, TSC decided removal and DHS TRIP communicated that review had occurred without confirming the listing or supplying reasons. The revised process described in this opinion confirms the listing, offers a second letter identifying a criterion and potentially an unclassified summary, invites a response, and sends the material to TSC. TSC recommends a disposition and can be asked for more information. The TSA Administrator issues the final administrative order. The panel's jurisdictional holding follows the change in the identity of the decisionmaker under 49 U.S.C. § 46110.
- Slip pp. 11–16 trace the four plaintiffs decided on this appeal. Each contested the stated basis and requested more information or process. The court says none submitted supporting evidence with the response. The Acting TSA Administrator kept each of the four listed. The opinion gives different levels of detail among their disclosures and expressly disavows treating the allegations as proved. Statements about other plaintiffs' status do not establish why their status changed.
- Slip pp. 40–43 distinguish a stated criterion from a full explanation. The panel's two conditions for withholding a reason involve classified information genuinely implicating national security and the lack of reasonable mitigation consistent with security. It describes review of the withheld material, justification and alternatives. The court's finding about sufficient process or nonprejudicial error is limited to these plaintiffs, not verification of the factual allegations or a holding on every later case (pp. 55–59).

The case strengthens the ordinary public-law comparator: dates, decision authority, reason coverage, the response opportunity and review of withheld materials already have doctrinal treatment. For NOMOS, same named case, notice sender and final authority must be coded separately. The 2019 procedural description cannot identify the original issuer of a missing individual document. Do not fill the authority of Fikre's 2016 delisting from the organization passing on the news or from the general rule alone.
