# NOMOS-0.885 P2-P4 — Inference Derivability versus Institutional Claim Authority
Date: 2026-11-05. Research type: synthetic countermodel and negative innovation audit; not real person adjudication.

## Motivating result from three actual primary PDFs
Barth et al. (2006) §3.1 explicitly defines computation rules on agent knowledge, so prior NOMOS language that older context-integrity formalisms lack *inference* is wrong. Nissenbaum (2004) III.A/B already distinguishes context-appropriate information from permitted distribution. Sultan & Jensen (2021) §3.2 and §4.1 already enforce inherited purpose constraints in aggregated resources. These results sharply narrow P2 originality claims.

## Model
Let `Know(a,p,t)` represent actor a's derived knowledge of attribute t of person p; `Flow(s,a,p,t,c)` represents permissioned information transfer in context c; `Judge(a,p,Q,u,τ)` denotes a later institutionally issued proposition Q for use u at time τ. The study asks whether a distinct `Warrant(a,p,Q,u,τ,rule,contestability)` is needed to license Judge. The existence of `Know` or `Flow` cannot alone prove a particular legal rule authorizes Q.

## Paired-world demonstration
Use identical synthetic message, context, derivable attribute, legal source-pointer and source transmission permission. In world A a receiving agency makes a narrower authorized case-specific claim with a named scope/warrant; in world B it asserts an institution-wide character classification without a matched warrant. The toy gate labels them differently given the added adjudication predicates. If adjudication inputs are hidden, information-flow observations alone cannot identify which classification action occurred.

This is **not an impossibility theorem** about Barth's logic, which can be extended with classification event types and additional deontic predicates. The criterion can be expressible by contextual integrity, purpose-limited provenance and standard legal review. No scientifically unique mechanism has been proven.

## Evidential controls

The public Supreme Court cases in P2-P1 remain source examples; no case provides the toy's actual judgment events. Thus no empirical detection metrics.

### P2-P4.7 — Observational-equivalence statement
Fix the observed transmission and inferable attribute π_flow(w)=(Flow,Know). Worlds A and B have equal π_flow by fixture design but differ in a later institutional claim. Consequently the observation projection cannot identify which *decision* was issued unless the decision event is independently observed. This is an elementary projection fact, not a novel representation theorem.


### P2-P4.8 — Expressibility defeater
Barth §3.1 already models computed attributes; the paper's core norm grammar centers communication events. An extension can add judge(a,p,Q,u) to the trace alphabet and prohibit unwarranted classifications using negative context-specific temporal norms. NOMOS cannot claim its toy separation is formally inexpressible in contextual-integrity extensions; no such inexpressibility proof exists.


### P2-P4.9 — Normative fact source missing

It does not verify who has legitimate claim authority, a legal provision granting it, actual notice, opportunity to contest or independent harms. Rule provenance and admissible institution-purpose scope must be directly grounded before making any legal assertion.
