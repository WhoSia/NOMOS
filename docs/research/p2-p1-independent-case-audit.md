# NOMOS-0.885 P2-P1 — Independent institutional authority-transfer case audit
Date: 2026-11-05. P2 falsification research; no novel proposition or empirical edge result certified.

## Research question
Does evidence custody or authorized flow entail authority for a *specific person-classifying proposition*? Do not confuse access, dissemination, contextual permissible use, legal standing and causally observed consequences.

## Primary legal cases
1. DOJ v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, 489 U.S. 749 (1989). FBI rap sheets compiled from publicly occurring records can invoke FOIA Exemption 7(C) privacy; FOIA central purpose is scrutiny of agency conduct, NOT the requester's own use purpose. The Court's FOIA requester identity rule is crucial negative guard. https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/489/749
2. NASA v. Nelson, 562 U.S. 134 (2011). Employment background questions under federal contractor program did not violate assumed informational privacy right; Court relied on employment interests and Privacy Act dissemination safeguards. This is a **legitimate collection** counterexample, not a categorical prohibition. https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/562/134/
3. TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez, 594 U.S. 413 (2021). Third-party dissemination for 1853 with misleading alerts versus no third-party disclosure for 6332 during class period mattered for Article III concrete harm in federal damages action; not generic proof that every internal error is legally harmless, nor all individuals achieved redress. https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/20-297

### P2-P1.6 — FOIA requester-purpose correction
DOJ v Reporters Committee (489 U.S. 749, at 771-775) rejects tailoring the FOIA disclosure interest to this particular requester's identity or personal reasons. It focuses on the Act's public interest in government-accountability information. A naive context-aware NOMOS algorithm that denies a rap sheet only because the requester is an employer would reproduce the *wrong rationale*. Contrast the government's compiled record and the public-interest basis rather than invented employment-purpose rules.


### P2-P1.7 — Permissible collection positive control
NASA v Nelson (562 U.S. 134) explicitly assumes without deciding a constitutional informational-privacy right and rejects the particular contractor background-investigation challenge in light of government's employment interest and Privacy Act protections. It is a positive control showing that protecting personhood cannot imply forbidding every inquiry. Whether statutory safeguards actually operated for a named worker is not established by the Court's general holding.


### P2-P1.8 — Standing versus merits
TransUnion v Ramirez (594 U.S. 413) distinguished 1,853 disseminated reports from 6,332 internal files for Article III standing on a particular FCRA claim. A claim that non-disseminated records cannot ever have legal consequences extends beyond that ruling. A claim that dissemination proves changed downstream employment or lending outcome also exceeds the ruling.


### P2-P1.9 — Stronger rivals than provenance alone
Nissenbaum 2004 contextual integrity, Barth/Datta/Mitchell/Nissenbaum 2006 transmission norms with temporal logic, and Sultan/Jensen 2021 provenance-enabled purpose limitation already encode combinations of actors, context, recipient roles, purpose, transmission rules and provenance. They constitute directly competing solutions to purported P2 originality. An incremental claim would require person-classification inference authority *after* an otherwise authorized flow, where those rivals and a trained legal reviewer demonstrably fail.
