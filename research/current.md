# NOMOS-0.885 — Current research, P2-P39

NOMOS asks when an institution can carry an observation forward as an authorized judgment about a person, and what a later correction changes for each recipient. Retention, disclosure, present use, authority to decide and observable consequences require distinct evidence. ## P2-P37 — Evidence ceilings, not case-count expansion

## P2-P38 — Philosophical origin and legitimacy of person-wide inference

The newly read [Rider (2006) and Tripodi (2016)](../docs/research/p2-p38-philosophical-professionalism-and-person-judgment.md) clarify the difference between *genuine philosophical problems and disciplinary evaluation*, and between *epistemic credibility and socially distributed recognition*. Fricker (1998, 2007) and Croce/Baghramian (2024) already address authority, testimony and expert standing. A methodologically justified criticism is not necessarily an institutional injustice; a local critique does not independently warrant a whole-person classification. Rival scholarship rules out novelty claims based on either observation alone.

The research program returns to *L'Étranger* and its action-to-person inference while retaining institutional cases only where they discriminate rival explanations. In this intake, four court PDFs duplicate already held originals **byte-for-byte**, three are alternate derivative copies, and two are distinct philosophy papers. Repeated PDF custody does not expand the sample of independent facts. The full private movement/custody status and refreshed missing-evidence list are recorded in the canonical Notion research page rather than creating a new public audit ledger.

## P2-P39 — Strong-rival test and corrected literature status

[Original-source comparison](../docs/research/p2-p39-person-inference-strong-rivals.md) distinguishes three non-identical mechanisms: persistence after *factual debunking* (Ross 1975; Anderson et al. 1980), residual influence of *legally inadmissible, possibly true* evidence (Steblay et al. 2006; Wistrich et al. 2005), and reinterpretation of counterexamples under *person/group traits* (Hayden–Mischel 1976; Kunda–Oleson 1995). Holyoak–Simon (1999) already explains bidirectional decision coherence; Fricker and Dotson already explain credibility and professional discipline. None establishes, in a specific legal case, that a corrected record reached the next decisionmaker and that the original invalid reason was reused.

The [Steblay 2006 full paper](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1VPWCPr_PTmO5nPnaN-T68_4Ema5Y0HKm/view) is now **HELD** in Research OS 10_PAPERS. Earlier Ross, Anderson, Kunda, Hayden and Holyoak originals and [Dotson 2012](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tjxXVQicDgAI4WlqnVDsqC1wSCRjUdR-/view) are also confirmed held. The generic historical [P2-P37 missing register](../docs/research/p2-p37-missing-evidence-register.md) has explicit P2-P39 status corrections; the authoritative present inventory is the private Notion study record.

[Study 1 page recheck](../docs/research/p2-p25-ko-study-1.md) confirms Ko 2016 printed p. 27 method N=92 and p. 32 Table 4 N=124, while p. 29 states a four-level design and p. 31 reports F(3,120). A hypothesized extra neutral cohort is **not proven** by that arithmetic, and it is wrong to call the dissertation itself missing. The next gap is the participant allocation explanation, if one exists. The [synthetic negative-control tests](../tests/test_p2P39DecisionPairNegativeControls.mjs) exercise same-outcome independent reasons, withdrawn-source derivation, absence of a second act, changed authority and incomplete reasons; these are regression checks, not claims about a real person's post-correction outcome.

**Research direction:** re-center the original Camus problem of act-relative versus person-wide warrant; keep legal case files as carefully bounded analogues, test ordinary source/provenance and legal analyses with the same primary texts, and do not promote beyond NOMOS-0.885 without a successful independent strong-rival test.
