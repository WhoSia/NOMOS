## The P0 research contract
Use different time axes: time information was received; time rule was operative; time decision issued; time appeal lodged/stay obtained; time legal interpretation became authoritative in a particular system; time a new law took effect (including any *actually enacted* retroactive effect); time of downstream correction. Do not equate a later judicial interpretation with a new statutory amendment. Distinguish contemporaneous reasonableness and validity under governing law, actual institutional actions, available legal remedies, and observed consequences.
Synthetic rivals:
A: Agency appeals adverse interpretation, obtains court-ordered stay with stated scope and review timetable; legal status and permitted actions differ from appeal without a stay.
B: Agency appeals but does not obtain a stay; administrative delay and ignoring operative orders may impose an independent burden despite agency confidence.
C: Higher court changes the legal interpretation; open future cases, live appeals, time-barred finalized cases and retrospective statute each need separately justified routes. No universal auto-reopen.
D: Underlying legislation remains in force but an agency knowingly administers a nonconforming rule while hoping for later legislation to validate it. A legislative proposal is not enacted retroactive authority.
E: An interim pause protects one population but delays lawfully payable benefits for another; distributional consequences and third-party reliance require independent inquiry.
Historical mechanism proof, individual relief and policy-change causal attribution remain open. No new automatic legal engine is called for merely to enumerate states.

## P2 — Legal finality and retrospective validation: decisive primary law contact (2026-10-10)
**Correct an earlier source-status assumption.** At NOMOS 0.878 P0–P1, the January 2026 Ombudsman report and November 2025 executive statement were only proof of a *proposed* amendment. Parliament later enacted **Social Security and Other Legislation Amendment (Technical Changes No. 1) Act 2026, No. 30**, assented to **1 April 2026**, with **Schedule 1 commencing 2 April 2026**. This is legislation, not merely policy intention. Official registered text: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00030/asmade/2026-04-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html . The correction does not retroactively change what the Ombudsman could report in January 2026.
**Read both validation pathways separately:** Schedule 1 Part 1 items 8–10 address child-support-period timing. Item 10(3) retroactively validates specified period-handling actions to the stated extent even if accrued rights affected, but 10(6) excepts rights/liabilities between parties to court proceedings *heard and finally determined before 2 April 2026*, within defined scope.
**Less-than-35%-care pathway** Schedule 1 Part 2 items 11–17: item 16(1) applies amendments to relevant assessments made/amended from **1 July 2008**; item 16(2) exempts specified past non-nil assessments, but item 16(3) re-applies amendments to objections/reviews decided after commencement; **item 16(4)** excepts specified rights/liabilities from proceedings already heard and finally determined by a court before commencement. **Item 17(1)-(2)** validates specified past nil-rate administration despite an effect on accrued rights, but **item 17(3)** protects specified court-finalised rights/liabilities from the validation. "Valid despite this defect" does not mean valid for all other reasons or all persons' remedies extinguished. Do not conflate a final ART review with a final court proceeding.
**Constructive pair:** C1 and C2 share child-support period, policy practice and factual history, but one has a relevant court proceeding finally heard/determined before 2 April, the other only a pending objection. Application of the statutory exception can differ. C3 has an objection/review *decided after* commencement; item 16(3) may move it into new-law applicability though the underlying assessment preceded enactment. C4 contains error on a different ground; the statutory "merely because" validation does not automatically cure it. These are statutory *applicability candidates*, not verified case outcomes.
