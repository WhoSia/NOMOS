# NOMOS-0.856 — Interim Consequence Suspension and Queue Debt
## Core question
How does a validly reopened historical person-judgment receive interim protection when review resources are insufficient, without prematurely deciding its merits?

## Four distinct authorities
- admissibility to historical recall;
- remedial sequencing and review;
- temporary protection pending review;
- final person judgment and consequence.
None implies another. **INTERIM PROTECTION IS NOT MERITS ADJUDICATION.**

## Protected explanandum
**INSTITUTIONAL WAITING TIME MUST NOT BECOME AN UNREVIEWED NEW ADVERSE PERSON-JUDGMENT.**
Delay is not always harm: it can become a material independent consequence when loss continues, time-limited options expire, or eventual reversal becomes ineffective.

## Contested decisions
An institution must make and log a bounded choice among status-quo maintenance, temporary access, suspending an adverse effect, escrow, notice-only and reasoned nonintervention. No universal automatic suspension rule is claimed. A remedy's costs to others, reversal and legal scope remain independent inputs.

## Dual failure
- **Delay laundering**: institution claims repair pending, but continuing adversity is never reviewed, accumulated repair debt goes unaccounted, and queue exhaustion becomes practical finality.
- **Protection-as-merits laundering**: provisional protection is misreported as evidentiary vindication, substantive reversal, permanent entitlement or precedent for unrelated persons.

## Formal
Let C be a validated recall cohort, q_i(t) review-queue status, a_i(t) continued adverse consequences, m_i(t) a temporary measure and W_i a future final merits finding.
A measure's adoption is valid only if grounded in an independent interim warrant I_i (including scope, counterparty effects, temporality and appeal).
It does not determine W_i, and absence of review does not validate a_i(t).

Remedial queue debt D_i(t) records outstanding material review/notice/compensation tasks and ongoing effects during waiting. Its recorded amount is not a scalar guilt or character score. Suspensions may protect against irreversible loss but require periodic review, notice and clean expiration rules.
