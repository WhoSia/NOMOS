- Paragraphs 12 and 16–18, pp.9–11: distinguish substituted merits-review decisions and relevant legal interpretations from case-specific factual findings. The latter cannot simply be assumed in subsequent cases. An agency should not ignore a review result in its own case; future legal interpretation is conditioned on actual law, appeal, stay and statutory framework.
- Paragraphs 57–60, printed pp.26–27: systematic intake, analysis, communication, training and policy revision where warranted. ARC expressly says these are **not new ideas**, citing its own 1995 recommendations, Robodebt Royal Commission, and 2024 Ombudsman work.

The Council reports that the final judicial outcome supported DSS's statutory interpretation; this is a **rival to the notion that every successful tribunal legal interpretation persists unchanged**.

## P0 — Typed event, warrant and response rather than a scalar duty
For an accepted case correction e, record source kind (binding court order and interpretation; tribunal substitution/remittal; tribunal-specific factual finding; validated technical shared-component defect; contested complaint; inquiry recommendation), issue/holding scope, legal status and stay/appeal posture, material shared mechanism, affected decision frame, source and recipient time, competence to reconsider a rule, and affected-party rights. A tuple is a dossier requirement, NOT an algorithm that generates legal obligations.

Separate responses:
(1) comply in source case where legally required;
(2) analyze precedent and possible shared mechanism;
(3) notify an appropriate policy/legal authority and give reasons;
(4) consider interim protection or lawful appeal/stay;
(5) revise prospective common rule where supported and authorized;
(6) investigate exposed cohort where scope bridge is supported;
(7) give individualized opportunity to be heard, redecide facts and verify consequences.
Case correction alone does NOT entail all seven. Conversely case-bound relief may co-exist with independent legal duties that already apply to future cases.

### Adversarial counterworlds
A — a finding depends on missing or contradictory claimant-specific documents, with no common defect or statutory holding. Local correction is warranted, generalized factual reversal is not.
B — a validated defect necessarily touches a shared income-computation component used across an identifiable historical cohort. A proportionate exposure audit is warranted by an established institution-level mechanism; individual outcomes still require their own record.
C — a competent appellate court authoritatively interprets a statute applicable to later cases. Prospective administrative compliance is supported by applicable court authority; this must not be reduced to a discretionary sample-size threshold.
D — the government takes a lawful appeal or stay; interim policy options and transparency require jurisdiction-specific evaluation. A pending appeal is not an unlimited license to ignore a legally operative decision.
E — a decision is set aside for an individual factual reason while identical policy wording appears elsewhere; same wording is not proof of common erroneous fact or exposure.
F — a complaint reveals a plausible common issue but evidence is insufficient to validate it. Preservation/investigation may be appropriate; cohort merits reversal is unwarranted.
G — an appeal succeeds under an older policy version, while current versions use independently corrected logic. Temporal, implementation and policy lineage are essential to scope.
