## P0 — Typed event, warrant and response rather than a scalar duty
For an accepted case correction e, record source kind (binding court order and interpretation; tribunal substitution/remittal; tribunal-specific factual finding; validated technical shared-component defect; contested complaint; inquiry recommendation), issue/holding scope, legal status and stay/appeal posture, material shared mechanism, affected decision frame, source and recipient time, competence to reconsider a rule, and affected-party rights. A tuple is a dossier requirement, NOT an algorithm that generates legal obligations.

Separate responses:
(1) comply in source case where legally required;
(2) analyze precedent and possible shared mechanism;
(3) notify an appropriate policy/legal authority and give reasons;
(4) consider interim protection or lawful appeal/stay;
(5) revise prospective common rule where supported and authorized;
(6) investigate exposed cohort where scope bridge is supported;
(7) give individualized opportunity to be heard, redecide facts and verify consequences.
Case correction alone does NOT entail all seven. Conversely case-bound relief may co-exist with independent legal duties that already apply to future cases.

### Adversarial counterworlds
A — a finding depends on missing or contradictory claimant-specific documents, with no common defect or statutory holding. Local correction is warranted, generalized factual reversal is not.
B — a validated defect necessarily touches a shared income-computation component used across an identifiable historical cohort. A proportionate exposure audit is warranted by an established institution-level mechanism; individual outcomes still require their own record.
C — a competent appellate court authoritatively interprets a statute applicable to later cases. Prospective administrative compliance is supported by applicable court authority; this must not be reduced to a discretionary sample-size threshold.
D — the government takes a lawful appeal or stay; interim policy options and transparency require jurisdiction-specific evaluation. A pending appeal is not an unlimited license to ignore a legally operative decision.
E — a decision is set aside for an individual factual reason while identical policy wording appears elsewhere; same wording is not proof of common erroneous fact or exposure.
F — a complaint reveals a plausible common issue but evidence is insufficient to validate it. Preservation/investigation may be appropriate; cohort merits reversal is unwarranted.
G — an appeal succeeds under an older policy version, while current versions use independently corrected logic. Temporal, implementation and policy lineage are essential to scope.

## P1 — Case outcome is not a sufficient statistic for institutional rule response (2026-10-10)
The 2026 ARC Statement gives a sharper test than "successful appeal triggers correction." It describes the Chaplin / Re FTXB litigation: the Full Federal Court found the AAT had erred about the statutory income-apportionment interpretation while also concluding that the AAT's actual debt outcome remained correct; the High Court on 17 June 2026 confirmed DSS's interpretation ([2026] HCA 22). *Case outcome and controlling reason can diverge*; different procedural remedies/statuses are time-indexed. The Statement's 2026 legal guidance is not retroactive proof of what any officer knew earlier.
### Minimal two-pair countermodel (abstract)
Let Y be whether one person's ultimate outcome changed. Let Q be the source kind and operative reason, J its binding/legal significance, M the material exposure bridge, and A the competent institutional authority.
- W1: Y=0 and a binding appellate statutory interpretation changes the permissible interpretation of a rule used in future cases. A relevant prospective agency response can be required even though the case outcome is unchanged.
- W2: Y=0 and no legal/rule defect is found. No identical prospective response follows merely from the unchanged outcome.
- W3: Y=1 because a unique claimant's missing document was produced and accepted. The case correction is valid; absent shared defect it does not compel cohort-wide merits correction.
- W4: Y=1 and independently validated common component defect with evidence of material cohort exposure exists. 0.854 and Abramov already warrant investigation of exposure scope (subject to controlling law), but individualized merits remain open.
W1/W2 and W3/W4 share the same Y but have materially different permissible institutional action sets. Thus **no rule based only on a single case's success/failure flag Y can consistently select the appropriate cross-case response for all four worlds**. This is an elementary insufficiency witness, not a new mathematical law.
### Authority-typed institutional response
Keep distinct: (a) case-specific compliance; (b) source-type and authoritative reason analysis; (c) acknowledgment and appropriate policy/legal escalation; (d) lawful appeal, stay or transparent bounded pause; (e) prospective common-rule revision when legal/technical predicates support it; (f) exposed-cohort investigation and sequencing; (g) individualized fact/reason assessment and observed downstream relief. **Escalation may be warranted without already being entitled to decide all other merits.** Conversely a legally controlling interpretation is not optional merely because a statistical defect cohort has not been reconstructed.
Three authority mistakes to audit: RISK-PREVALENCE-AS-LAW (requiring prevalence estimate before complying with applicable statutory interpretation), PERSON-FACT-AS-PRECEDENT (transferring a case-specific fact to other people), INSTITUTIONAL-SIMILARITY-AS-EXPOSURE (same agency rather than a proven shared material mechanism).
