## P0 typed claims: four non-substitutable evidence channels
L(t,d) = operative/legal status of decision d after a later Act and any qualifying exception; H(t_0,d) = dated historical compliance and notice record at time of action; Q(p,t) = identifiable, legally available and practically reachable review/compensation route for person p; Y(p,t) = actual person-linked restoration supported by independent readback. Additional X(frame,t) = screened population exposure with frame and denominator.
L is no proof of H, Q or Y; X alone cannot estimate attrition to Q or Y. The opposite is also important: H describing failure cannot resolve modern legal merits, and Q accessibility cannot certify completed compensation. No universal inference that courts disregard savings statutes or that CDDA automatically compensates affected parties.

### Medical case B — Singh et al. 2010 lab alerts (diagnostic follow-up, no injury verdict)
Singh et al. *American Journal of Medicine* 2010, DOI **10.1016/j.amjmed.2009.07.027**, original publication at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20193832/ and manuscript at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2878665/ . Out of **78,158 tests**, **1,163 high-priority electronic alerts**, **119 not acknowledged** and **79 without timely follow-up** after chart review and contacting providers (30 days). Alerts are not unique patients; 119 unacknowledged != 79 lacking timely follow-up. Additional review of initial missing-documentation flags changed the error classification. This is a real retrospective alert audit, NOT patient-level proof of injury, restoration or legal negligence.

### Medical case C — Singh et al. 2009 imaging alerts
Singh et al. *Archives of Internal Medicine* 2009, DOI **10.1001/archinternmed.2009.263**, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19786677/ . **123,638 imaging studies**, **1,196 critical alerts**, **217 unacknowledged**, **92 without timely follow-up at four weeks**; communication acknowledgment and actual recommended clinical action are not equivalent. Subsequent provider contact triggered some action, not proof of independent health recovery. Unlike intervention RCT, this is observational follow-up research.

### Medical case D — Atlas et al. 2023 cluster randomized trial
Atlas et al. *JAMA* 2023, DOI **10.1001/jama.2023.18755**, https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2810508 . **11,980 people, 44 practices**; four treatment arms: EHR+outreach+navigation n3455, 120-day **adjusted** follow-up completion 31.4%; EHR+outreach n2569, 31.0%; EHR-only n3254, 22.7%; usual care n2702, 22.9%. The reported adjusted absolute difference **+8.5 percentage points** for full package vs usual care is evidence of a study-relative randomized effect on ***follow-up completion***, not demonstrated improvement in cancer-specific survival or whole-person recovery. Cannot multiply the adjusted percentages by n to recover exact observed events. Patient letter/phone attempts are not proof all participants received or understood the messages.

### Medical case E — Coronado et al. 2025 patient navigation trial
Coronado et al. *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2025, DOI **10.7326/ANNALS-24-01885**, https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-24-01885 . Randomized n985, primary modified ITT n967 (navigation 479; usual care 488). One-year adjusted follow-up colonoscopy completion **55.1% vs 42.1%**, reported difference **+13.0 percentage points** (95% CI 6.5–19.4). Receipt of the allocated navigation differs from random assignment. Patient-level data are explicitly not distributed by the authors in the paper's data-sharing statement. This study supports a bounded intervention effect on test completion, not on death or disability. Even a well-supported RCT effect does not establish a particular person's successful follow-up.

### Medical case F — Corley et al. 2017 follow-up-time cohort
Corley et al. *JAMA* 2017, DOI **10.1001/jama.2017.3634**, https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2620087 . **106,520** positive FIT tests at preliminary screening, **81,518** eligible analysis frame, **70,124** received follow-up colonoscopy and comprised the cancer stage analysis. Among recipients, follow-up after 10 months was associated with more diagnosed colorectal cancer and advanced stage compared with 8–30 days. Differences are observational and the association is NOT independently proven causal; excluded/never-colonoscoped people prevent naive extrapolation to every person with a positive FIT. Do not issue clinical timing advice from this study.

### Five falsifiers / observation limits
1. EHR alert 'acknowledged' but clinician follow-up unperformed => observed acknowledgment is not a closed loop.
2. Patient contacted and diagnostic follow-up completed, but downstream biological outcomes unknown => *process success* without proven *health recovery*.
3. Trial reports adjusted follow-up proportions and matched cohort sizes, but no raw patient outcome data => no invented event counts.
4. Population screening count and a subset of completed colonoscopies use different eligibility frames => no automatic selection-free cohort prevalence or causal estimate.
5. Agency file corrected and publicly advertised CDDA option available, but no delivered notice, application decision, payment or independently read-back claimant consequence => documentary repair != person restoration.
