### Australian controlling text — C2026A00030, Schedule 1
Assent 2026-04-01; Schedule 1 commenced 2026-04-02. Statutory PDF (pp.8–10 in printed pagination): https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00030/asmade/2026-04-01/text/original/pdf
- Item 16(1): relevant assessments made or amended on/after 1 July 2008 (not every assessment whatever its subject).
- Item 16(2): the pre-commencement exception requires *all* specified conditions: made/amended before commencement; rate calculated under s35 or s37; care of one parent under 35%; annual amount payable by the other parent greater than nil.
- Item 16(3): a post-commencement decision on an objection or ART review relating to such an assessment may be governed by the amendments despite item 16(2). Timing of the *review decision*, not only the original assessment, matters.
- Item 16(4): protects *to the extent* of rights/liabilities from court proceedings heard and finally determined *before* commencement and related to the assessment. An ART-only final result or an unrelated court judgment is not automatically this exception.
- Item 17(1)–(2): retrospective validation limited to the described past nil-rate determination because another parent's care was under 35%; it may affect accrued rights. This is *not* a blanket repair or validation of unrelated defects.
- Item 17(3): exception for the specified rights/liabilities arising between parties to court proceedings heard and finally determined before commencement insofar as related to that nil-rate ground.
- Separate item 10(6) addresses a **different** period-end error in Part 1 and should not be merged into item 16/17.

### Cross-ecology genuine legal counterexample — New Zealand 2026 No 6
Official enacted text: https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2026/6/en/latest/
Inserted Schedule 1 Part 11 clause 104 defines changeover = **2026-02-17 14:00** local to New Zealand. Clauses 107–108 preserve specific **proceedings begun before changeover**, even if not finally determined. The legal subject is different: income attribution and accident-compensation interaction under NZ Social Security Act 2018. It refutes any proposed universal rule that legislative savings are necessarily triggered only by final court judgment. It does not disprove or supersede Australian law.

## P1 — Counterworlds and typed authority boundaries
A: identical old nil-rate assessment, but a relevant court action finally heard/determined before 2026-04-02 in one world versus an ART objection not yet decided in another. Only the former presents a statutory court-finality *candidate*; the result for neither individual is automatically determined.
B: same pre-commencement positive assessment and care percentage; objection decided on 2026-04-01 vs 2026-04-03. Item 16(3) may separate applicability (subject to the full item 16(2) predicates and relationship). Neither original date nor appeal filing alone suffices.
C: two pre-commencement court judgments both final, but only one sets rights/liabilities connected to the validated child-support issue. Unrelated finality cannot be borrowed.
D: old nil-rate administrative decision suffers the specified under-35% defect and a separate independent defect. Validation in item 17(1) cannot be mechanically inferred to cure the independent defect.
E: two identical assessments have different observed downstream losses or restoration receipts. Retrospective legal status is not evidence that a person's material world has been restored.
F: New Zealand proceedings started before vs after 17 Feb at 14:00, both pending on 11 March; clause 108 may save the former, contrasting with Australian court-finality criterion. **No cross-jurisdictional direct individual legal comparability is assumed**.
