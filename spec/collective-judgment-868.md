# NOMOS-0.868 — Collective Consensus and Judgment Authority

**CLOSED (bounded conceptual court; 2026-10-09).** Anderson (2006), *The Epistemology of Democracy*, DOI 10.3366/epi.2006.3.1-2.8, analyzes inclusive information, dissent, collective learning and feedback. Sunstein (2002), *The Law of Group Polarization*, DOI 10.1111/1467-9760.00148, investigates why deliberation can amplify existing orientations. The supplied Sunstein PDF is text-extraction corrupted, so its discussion is grounded in publisher/reference materials rather than a verified full-text close read.

**Countermodel:** identical numbers of agreeing observers can come from independent evidence plus open correction or from shared-source conformity without independent grounds. Even epistemically warranted criticism of an act does not alone authorize group sanctions or global moral judgment. The community may also properly challenge injustices: no blanket dismissal of collective critique.

Earlier NOMOS 0.839, 0.865, 0.866 and 0.867 anticipate these issues. No new theorem, empirical social frequency or real-person judgment is certified.

**0.869 title proposal only:** Dissent as Evidence, Interpretive Disobedience, Collective Epistemic Closure & the Primitive Question of When Contesting a Community's Judgment Is Misread as Proof That the Community Was Right.
