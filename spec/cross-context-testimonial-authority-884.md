## P0-R1 — McKeon primary opinion and explicit boundaries (2026-10-17)
Source: United States v. McKeon, 738 F.2d 26 (2d Cir. 1984), https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/738/26/135199/ . Court rejects categorical exclusion of a lawyer's earlier opening but imposes stringent conditions for later *evidentiary* use: clear inconsistency of factual claims, not merely speculation or arguments; a sufficient basis to treat the earlier proposition as the client's testimonial equivalent, not agency alone; and meaningful handling of competing explanations, with concerns about privilege, strategy and counsel disqualification. The distinction between a binding judicial admission within a case and a prior evidentiary admission is explicit in the opinion. This is existing Second Circuit doctrine, not a general person-character law or a Korean rule.
Internal reuse: 0.246 authorized delegation does not license arbitrary new scope; 0.261 correction-burden lens reveals costs of contest; 0.724–0.798 copied enduring predicates; 0.881 bounded closure; Greenberg 2009 warns apparent corroboration may share origins. Positive witness: a well-grounded clear factual inconsistency can be properly considered when safeguards hold. Negative witness: generic counsel speculation is not automatically the defendant's factual assertion.
